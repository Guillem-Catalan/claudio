## PAE — ROLE CONTEXT

The PAE (Partner Account Executive) takes over once the PBD has
qualified a prospect and booked a demo. From that point, the PAE
owns the full sales cycle: demo, discovery, objection handling,
pricing negotiation, and close.

The PAE operates within a partner channel — every prospect is an
existing client of Santander, Telefónica, or MEO. This gives the
PAE a warm entry and a trust shortcut that must be actively used.
A PAE who ignores the partner relationship is throwing away the
main competitive advantage of this channel.

The PAE's job is not to present features. It is to understand the
business impact of the prospect's problems, build a champion who
will sell Factorial internally, identify who controls the budget
and decision, and create a concrete path to signature.

---

## EVALUATION FRAMEWORK — MEDDIC

MEDDIC is a deal-level framework. It accumulates across all calls —
a pillar confirmed in a previous call remains confirmed unless new
information contradicts it. When deal history is provided, evaluate
each pillar in the context of everything learned so far, not just
what happened in this call.

Each pillar is evaluated as:
- **Confirmed** — explicit, validated evidence in the transcript
  or previous calls
- **Partial** — mentioned or partially explored but not fully
  qualified
- **Missing** — not raised in any interaction so far
- **N/A** — not applicable to this call type

The six pillars:

**Metrics** — Has the PAE quantified the business impact?
Not "it will save you time" — that is Partial at best. Confirmed
requires: hours/month saved, cost of payroll errors in €, headcount
avoided, or any concrete number the prospect has verbalized.

**Economic Buyer** — Is the actual budget signer identified?
Not the HR contact, not the champion — the person who signs the
contract. Has the PAE confirmed who that is and whether they are
engaged?

**Decision Criteria** — Are the mandatory requirements known?
What does the prospect need to say yes? Integrations, GDPR, IT
requirements, specific features, price ceiling?

**Decision Process** — Is the internal approval roadmap confirmed?
Who is involved, in what order, and what are the steps before
a contract can be signed? IT, Legal, Procurement, CEO?

**Champion** — Is there an internal ally actively selling Factorial
when the PAE is not in the room?
A champion is not just someone who likes Factorial. They must be
willing to defend it internally, have access to decision-makers,
and be actively engaged.

**Competition** — Has the competitive landscape been mapped?
Is the prospect evaluating alternatives? Known competitors: SAP,
Meta4, Kenjo, local gestoría tools. Has the PAE addressed the
comparison directly?

## DEAL HEALTH & FORECAST

Three critical questions — evaluated cumulatively across the deal:
1. Is Factorial among the functional favorites?
2. Is the price in an agreed range — not "in negotiations"?
3. Is there a specific signature date range — not "end of quarter"?

**GREEN** — Two or three confirmed
**AMBER** — One missing or one vague
**RED** — Two or three missing

Additional signals: champion identified and active, economic buyer
engaged, PAE has multi-threaded to at least 2 stakeholders.

---

## PARTNER LEVERAGE — PAE (1-4)

1 — Partner completely absent. Factorial presented as standalone
    with no reference to the partner relationship or discount.
2 — Partner mentioned only as referral source or to explain the
    discount. Not used to build trust or reduce friction.
3 — Partner actively woven in: discount as exclusive benefit,
    partner trust used to reduce adoption risk, partner context
    used to address objections.
4 — Partner used as deal accelerator: PAE references partner's
    stake in the deal, proposes looping in the partner contact
    to unblock internally, or uses partner deadlines to compress
    the decision timeline.

## DEAL LIFECYCLE — PAE

**PBD Handover** — The PAE receives a handover briefing with BANT
signals and key context. Must use this before the demo.

**Demo** — Usually starts with the champion (HR Manager, Operations).
The PAE runs full discovery + product walkthrough. In some deals,
the prospect requests a second demo with additional stakeholders
(CEO, CFO, IT, Legal) — this is a positive signal, not a setback.
If this happens, the PAE must arrive at the second demo knowing
exactly what each new stakeholder cares about, and MEDDIC gaps
from the first demo must be addressed.

**Follow Ups** — The PAE maintains momentum, resolves blockers,
strengthens the champion, and moves towards a concrete decision
timeline.

**Closing** — Explicit close attempt. Price confirmed, decision
criteria met, signature date on the table.

When deal history is provided, always evaluate the current call
in the context of where the deal is in this lifecycle. A Follow Up
after two demos is evaluated differently than one after a single demo.
