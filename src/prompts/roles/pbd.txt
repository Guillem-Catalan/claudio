## PBD — ROLE CONTEXT

The PBD (Partners Business Development) is the top of the funnel.
Their job is to qualify the lead and book a demo with a PAE.
They never discuss price, never run demos, and never go deep into
product features. If a prospect asks for pricing — the PBD deflects
and pivots to booking the demo. Their second job is to engage with the
partner sales force to generated the maximum amount of leads. 

The PBD always operates from a partner context — every prospect is
an existing client of Santander, Telefónica, or MEO. This is not a
cold call channel. The partner relationship is the PBD's main tool
to open doors and reduce resistance. A PBD who doesn't use it is
working much harder than they need to.

The PBD works a high volume of calls — many will be voicemails,
gatekeepers, or wrong contacts. That is normal. What matters is
execution quality on the calls that connect with a real prospect.

## EVALUATION FRAMEWORK — BANT

Like MEDDIC for PAE, BANT is a deal-level framework for PBD.
It accumulates across all calls to the same prospect — a pillar
qualified in a previous call remains valid unless contradicted.
When deal history is provided, evaluate BANT in the context of
everything learned so far.

Each pillar is evaluated as:
- **Confirmed** — explicit, validated evidence in the transcript
  or previous calls
- **Partial** — mentioned or touched on but not fully qualified
- **Missing** — not raised in any interaction so far

The four pillars:

**Budget** — Was pricing, budget ownership, or cost sensitivity
addressed? Did the rep correctly frame Factorial as a discounted
partner product? Understanding whether the prospect uses another
tool or is considering alternatives also counts as a budget signal
— even without a concrete number.

**Authority** — Did the rep identify who makes the decision? Is it
the person on the call or someone else? If the champion is not the
decision-maker, the rep should identify their role in the process
and who else needs to be involved.

**Need** — Did the rep uncover a specific, named pain point relevant
to Factorial's features? Not a generic "we have HR processes" — a
concrete problem: manual clock-ins, payroll errors, shift management
chaos, document chaos. The prospect must have verbalized it.

**Timing** — Is there a concrete timeframe — a trigger event, a
deadline, a moment of urgency? A time range is valid if no specific
date exists.

## SCRIPT COMPLIANCE

**Phase 1 — Opener**
Must reference the partner relationship. This is not a cold call —
the prospect is an existing client of the partner, and their
contact was shared directly by them.

- PASS: Partner explicitly mentioned + role of the contact
  referenced ("tu gestor de Santander", "tu comercial de Telefónica")
  + warm framing established
- FLAG: Partner mentioned but without warm framing or role reference
- FAIL: Generic cold call opener with no partner reference

Exception: if a prior touchpoint is clearly referenced (previous
event, joint call, email introduction) → explicit partner mention
is optional.

**Phase 2 — Industry Pivot**
Goal: reach a specific, named pain point for this prospect.
The sector pivot is one way to get there — not the only one.

- PASS: Rep identified the sector and pivoted to a vertical-specific
  pain. OR rep surfaced a concrete pain directly from the prospect's
  own words without needing a sector pivot.
- FLAG: Pivot attempted but pain was generic — not tailored to this
  company or situation.
- FAIL: No pivot, no specific pain identified. Same generic pitch
  regardless of who they're speaking to.

Sector reference:
- Construction / Agriculture → paper clock-ins, seasonal workers
- Healthcare / Hospitality → shift management, sick leave cover
- Transport / Logistics → expense reports, mileage tracking
- Consulting / IT / SaaS → performance reviews, ATS
- Retail → geolocation clock-ins, multi-location planning

**Phase 3 — Close**
The rep must secure a concrete next step — ideally a scheduled demo.

- PASS: Demo or concrete next step booked with specific date/time +
  confirmed + participants mentioned
- FLAG: Next step agreed but date/time vague or not confirmed
- FAIL: No next step, or open-ended "I'll send you info"

Key closing techniques to look for:
- Value Tie-Down: "¿Te merece la pena 45 minutos?"
- Two-Slot Close: exactly two specific time slots offered
- Calendar Anchor: "¿Tienes el calendario a mano?"

## PARTNER LEVERAGE — PBD (1-4)

1 — Partner not mentioned after opener, or mentioned only as
    a formality with no effect on the conversation
2 — Partner mentioned to explain the referral or discount,
    but not used to reduce resistance or build trust
3 — Partner used to frame the value proposition: discount as
    exclusive benefit, partner trust referenced to lower friction
4 — Partner actively leveraged to accelerate the booking: rep
    uses the partner contact's name, references a specific
    interaction with the partner, or creates urgency by linking
    the booking to the partner relationship

## RED FLAGS — PBD

Fire a Slack alert if any of these trigger:
- **BANT_3_MISSING** — 3 or more BANT pillars Missing on a
  connected call where qualification was possible
- **PARTNER_LEVERAGE_1** — partner_leverage_score = 1

## DEAL LIFECYCLE — PBD

**Early attempts** — Voicemails, no answers, wrong numbers,
gatekeepers. The goal is to reach the right person. Evaluate
execution quality, not outcome.

**First connection** — BANT begins to accumulate. The goal is to
qualify enough to justify a demo and attempt to book it.

**Subsequent connections** — BANT should progress with each
connected call. If a pillar remains Missing after two connected
calls, that is a gap.

**Demo booked (tag 91)** — The ideal outcome. Evaluate the quality
of the booking — was it a strong, confirmed close or a weak,
open-ended "send me info"?

**Partner calls (tag 991)** — Calls with the partner, not the
prospect. Evaluate strategic alignment and agreed next steps.

When deal history is provided, always evaluate the current call
in the context of how many times the PBD has already tried, what
has been qualified so far, and what was agreed in the last
interaction.
